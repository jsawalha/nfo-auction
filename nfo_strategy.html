QB — the single biggest edge in the league

The 20 biggest FAAB league-winners in league history are ALL quarterbacks. QBs are cheap at auction and the wire hands out a top-5 QB for $0–8 every single year (Rodgers $8→317, Maye $8→232, Mayfield $3→224, Cousins $1→189, Purdy $0→140).

Punt QB to ~$5–12 at the auction, then stream/claim off waivers. Basaraville Xtreme has quietly mastered this and won 2025. It is free points every year.

$30–44 is a desert — ~3.8 backs a year, and some years zero. (2023 had no RB at $40–44; 2022 had none at $30–39.) The market is barbell-shaped: ~10 anchors at $45+, a hole, then value at ≤$29. "I'll grab a solid RB2 for ~$35" is a plan that frequently has no inventory.

The tier to actively skip: $12–22 (RB14–30), median $18. That's a replaceable committee back at a price where the room overpays for name recognition. Commit to one side of the barbell — anchors at $45+ or sub-$10 targeted darts — and never budget for the middle. Note the cumulative 12 RBs at $40+ is exactly where the RB cliff sits.

Nomination-order value pockets

Does a player go cheaper depending on when in the run he's nominated? After controlling for talent (price minus what that ADP rank normally costs), almost everything is noise — with two real soft spots.

−$8
the ~10th RB off the board — the mid-run lull after the elite rush drains the aggressive wallets
−$6
the ~6th WR off the board — the pause before the WR-hoarders re-engage
−$0
effect of a positional run (RB1–3 back-to-back) on the next back — none
A run predicts nothing; remaining supply predicts everything. "RB1–3 just went, pounce on RB11" is a myth — he sells for exactly his model price. The real edge is the lull: a legit starter nominated after the opening stud rush but before the position's hoarders wake back up. Watch for an RB1–9-caliber back thrown out around the 5th–10th off the board.

Sticky stats & TD regression

Which stats actually predict next-year production — and which are variance you shouldn't pay for.

What predicts, what doesn't

  • WR: buy YAC + target share. Yards-after-catch is both sticky and forecasts next-year points better than the TDs themselves. Fade the low-catch-rate deep threat.
  • RB: buy volume & red-zone opportunity. Carries and RZ touches are equally predictive of next-year TDs and far more stable than the TDs. Project the workload, not the highlight reel.
  • Fade efficiency spikes and last year's TDs. RB yards-per-carry is ~zero-sticky; TDs regress hard (WR TD → next-year signal is negative). Don't overpay a back off a shiny YPC year or a receiver off a lucky TD count.

Market psychology — where the value leaks are

The biggest bargains aren't structural (timing/position runs are ~zero). They're psychological — the auction overpays for ceiling and fades uncertainty.

  • Buy the boring-floor workhorse the room fears. The biggest value leaks are RBs with a "cloud" — committee / age / contract narratives (Najee Harris RB9 ADP → $22, Mixon → −$16, Rhamondre → −$14). The market fears the committee; the volume-secure back keeps the workload anyway. Elite TEs leak every year (nobody wants to pay TE).
  • Don't buy the dead-cat name. Davante Adams for $6 wasn't a steal — his ADP had already collapsed to WR40 and the price just matched it. Before you pounce on a cheap star, check his ADP; if it already fell, the discount is earned, not a leak.
The rule: target the player with a scary narrative but a stable role (workhorse RBs tagged "committee," elite TEs nobody bids up). Fade the famous name whose ranking already dropped.

Handcuffs & the bench — the San Di Lello method

The champion runs a 7-RB build and fills the back half with a specific kind of cheap back.

  • Buy $2–10 targeted handcuffs, not $1 fliers. His cheap RBs cluster at $4–8 — the clear backup / ascending committee guy (Judkins, Warren, Charbonnet), not pure lottery tickets. Over 5 years he bought exactly one $1 RB (Bucky Irving — and it hit).
  • Self-handcuff your most fragile stud. CMC + Elijah Mitchell ($2), James Cook + Ray Davis ($3) — buy the direct backup so an injury to your anchor doesn't sink your season.
  • Don't marry the insurance. He drops the pure-depth handcuff around week 5–11 once the starter proves healthy, freeing the spot for a streamer. Handcuffs are insurance with a rent cost — cancel it when the risk clears.

Even the champion can't pick which $1 dart will hit — he drafted and dropped Shaheed, McConkey, and Adonai Mitchell in 2024. Darts are unknowable: buy several, keep the one that hits, cut the rest fast.

The waiver reality — churn ≠ success

In this league, waiver production and winning correlate at ~zero (slightly negative). Heavy churn is a symptom of a weak roster, not a cause of winning — San Di Lello ranks near last in waiver points and wins because his draft is strong.

What the successful waiver seasons actually do: stream QB & TE off the wire (you draft only 1 of each), and snipe the RB who just inherited a backfield (Rico Dowdle, Kimani Vidal, Jamaal Williams). Act on Wednesday (waiver processing) with Friday free-agent tweaks, and bid real FAAB ($7–45) on a genuine difference-maker — not $0 fliers. It's a scalpel for winners, not a crutch.

You currently have the most waiver volume in the league and finish 8th — you're churning to patch the draft. Fix the draft first; then use the wire surgically like the Kossaks (14.5 pts/pickup, top-3 finishes).

Live draft-day tactics

  • Count the paddles — bidder count is the strongest live signal (+0.41). 5+ bidders means ~$3 over model regardless of scarcity. Position runs (~0) and nomination timing tell you far less than how many hands are up.
  • Don't nominate QB or TE early expecting a discount — there isn't one. Early QB is flat-priced; early elite TE actually goes at a premium (+$8). The discount comes from waiting until wallets empty. Let a rival nominate the elite TE past the 20-minute mark, then pounce.
  • Read the depth tiers by age. At RB22-ish depth, the room pays up for young upside ($16–23) and fades aging vets ($8–10). TreVeyon at ~$17 is correctly priced as an upside dart, not a decline vet — fine at/below that, a reach toward $23.
  • Watch the Tier Watch bands. Amber (≤2 left) = act, red = move on. Buying above a cliff at model price beats buying below it at a discount — the cheaper player is materially worse.

Opponent scouting

San Di Lello — the dynasty (avg finish 2.6)

7-RB build (6.6 RB / 5.2 WR / 1 QB / 1 TE), stars-and-scrubs, streams QB/TE. Anchors on RB, pays a targeted tier premium on the back he wants, then recovers with discipline. Against him: don't get into an RB war on a mid-tier back he's locked onto. Note the natural experiment — 2024 WR-heavy → #1; 2025 RB-heavy with no WR alpha + a $22 mid-tier RB → collapsed to #6. Best single validation of this whole doc.

The Cats — the balanced counter (2× #2)

WR-leaning and flexible (5.4 RB / 6.4 WR), will pivot hard (3 RB / 9 WR in 2024). Their mid-tier WRs carry it (Pickens $20→106, Higgins $21→76) — consistent with the data: mid-tier WR good, mid-tier RB bad. Against them: expect them to bid up ascending mid-tier WRs.

Down by the Rivers — the sniper

Dual-RB anchor every year, the league's earliest buyer (Ekeler at minute 0/9/10 three straight years). Won't fight you for a second back, but will take a premium RB off the board before you've settled in.

ReD PantY NiTe — the fix (that's you)

Pours ~70% into RB (least-efficient position), starves WR, overpays most in the league (+$2.8 on studs), and churns the wire hardest — and finishes 8th–10th. The fix is one move repeated: cut ~$40 off RB → buy a real $35–45 WR alpha; hold your walk-away prices on studs; copy San Di Lello's structure (2–3 anchors, $2–10 targeted handcuffs, nothing in the middle). You're already good at cheap-QB streaming — lean into it.
